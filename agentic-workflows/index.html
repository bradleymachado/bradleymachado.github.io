01 / Two Commitments
01

Design for the person who inherits it.

I came to architecture from operations. I held the utility bill for a Silver-certified building constructed ninety degrees off its intended orientation, glass atrium facing full sun. Nobody who made that decision lived with it. The people who occupy, maintain, and pay for a building are the brief.

02

Run execution at agent scale.

I direct AI agents the way I ran construction: a written scope, bounded tasks, a report at each milestone, and an inspection before anything moves forward. It lets one designer carry software and data work alongside the design itself. Accountability does not transfer to the agent.

02 / Method
Brief

Define the user, the objective, and the constraints before any agent runs. An undefined objective is the most expensive failure mode in agentic work. Agents execute it quickly and confidently.

Decompose

Break the work into subtasks small enough to verify. Each gets its own instructions, inputs, and acceptance check. Independent subtasks run in parallel.

Gate

Every step that can change the next one ends in a report and a stop. Destructive or irreversible steps wait for a person. Nothing goes live on an agent's word alone.

Verify

Check output against the source, not against the agent's summary of it. On this site, drafted copy that claimed Revit on SketchUp projects was caught and pulled for exactly that reason.

Persist

Agents start each session cold. Decisions, conventions, and open items live in versioned build notes and plans, so the next session resumes from the record instead of from memory.

Position
